# Joint Strategy

## Plinth

- glue plus `3 in` structural screws
- predrill near ends
- keep rails square before adding caster reinforcement blocks
- check the plinth for twist before setting cabinets on it

## Cabinet Carcasses

- cabinet screws or Confirmat-style screws for main plywood box assembly
- glue at all long-grain plywood joints
- install backs only after the carcass is confirmed square
- predrill all screws that enter plywood edges
- use clearance holes in the driving face so the screws actually pull the joint together
- countersink only flat-head screws; washer-head screws should sit on the surface and not in a countersink

## Module Joining

- clamp modules flush
- use washer-head screws through doubled walls
- drill oversize clearance holes in the driving wall and lightly slot them vertically if the top edges need a little alignment range
- recheck top-edge flushness before tightening all fasteners

## Pilot And Clearance Rules

- `FAST-02` structural screws into lumber: predrill near ends and close to edges
- `FAST-03` washer-head cabinet screws: drill a clearance hole through the first panel and a pilot only where the screw enters an edge
- `FAST-11` pivot hardware holes: drill with a brad-point bit and a drill guide or drill press if possible so both side plates stay coaxial
- `FAST-13` latch screws and `FAST-16` block screws: predrill to the root diameter of the screw before driving into plywood

## Fixed Top

- glue the raw back of the prefinished wear skin to the raw substrate face with `HDW-06`
- do not glue the top permanently to the carcass
- attach the laminated top from below with accessible screws after the carcass is proven square
- keep screws out of future trim paths opening zones and router-plate ledger landings

## Saw Cradle

- ledgers screwed and glued to carcass sides
- adjustable mounting deck supported by `CM-05` carriers and jack screws
- jack screws provide fine adjustment
- saw-mount bolts drilled only after the actual saw is flush-fit on the deck

## Miter Station

- tray and cover parts can be cut from the public DCS781 envelope and layout opening without a bespoke prebuild survey
- pivot hardware is mechanical and adjustable
- deployed hard stops establish repeatable height
- final saw-surface flushness comes from a separate shim or spacer stack under the real saw feet
- stowed latches only pull the cover flush; they are not the deployed datum

## Service Panels And Hatches

- router hatch may use simple latches or magnets
- front service face uses removable machine screws into inserts for repeatable removal
- `RM-10` uses its own fasteners and disconnectable harness so `RM-06` is not trapped
