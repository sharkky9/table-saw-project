# Assembly-Mode Strategy

## Recommendation

Do not convert the permanent top into a full-time clamping grid on day one. In the sliding-carriage variant, the left field is now precision crosscut real estate.

Use a layered approach instead.

## Layer 1: Keep The Permanent Top Clean

- no fixed T-track in the left carriage field
- no proud hardware where sheet goods or the carriage fence need to travel
- no casual drilling into the carriage guide zone

This branch intentionally gives up the old far-left permanent T-tracks to protect the new carriage package.

## Layer 2: Use The Bench Surface Itself

For ordinary assembly:

- lower the blade fully
- install a blank or solid throat plate
- remove or park the sliding carriage if you need uninterrupted surface area
- remove the router fence
- use the fixed top as a large flat surface

This is already useful without routing the whole bench into a fixture plate.

## Layer 3: Future Removable Overlay

Plan a `54 x 36 x 3/4 in` removable overlay that:

- registers to the rear edge
- anchors only after the carriage is removed or parked out of the way
- starts with two low-conflict T-tracks
- can later be perforated with `20 mm` holes if you actually want MFT behavior
- carries an underside stiffener where it spans the saw opening so clamp loads do not sag the panel into the throat area

## What The Overlay Should Solve

- more clamp points than the permanent top should carry
- sacrificial track-saw work
- glue-up work where squeeze-out is expected
- experimentation with dogs and fences without risking the carriage field

## What Not To Do

- Do not place permanent T-track in the carriage field.
- Do not put proud hardware in the right-side rip-support field.
- Do not route the permanent top for a full dog-hole grid until you know the carriage variant is staying.
- Do not assume the overlay can float unsupported over the saw opening; it needs either the lowered saw top beneath it or its dedicated stiffener.
