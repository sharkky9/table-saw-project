# Finish Schedule

## Goal

This is shop furniture. The target is:

- good hand feel
- durable sealed surfaces
- easy cleanup
- no splintery edges

It is **not** a furniture-grade showpiece.

## Surface Classes

### Class A: Touch And Precision Surfaces

- fixed top
- sliding carriage
- left support table
- router module
- carriage fence
- service faces and frequently handled storage edges

Treatment:

- ease edges with a `1/16 to 1/8 in` roundover or chamfer
- sand to `150` then `180`
- seal MDF with dewaxed shellac
- apply `2 to 3` coats satin waterborne polyurethane

### Class B: Visible Working Surfaces

- outer cabinet faces
- service doors
- utility faces

Treatment:

- break edges
- sand to `150`
- apply one sealer coat plus one or two finish coats as time allows

### Class C: Hidden Structure

- plinth
- inside framing
- underside blocking

Treatment:

- knock down splinters
- spot-sand hand-contact edges
- optional quick seal coat in dust-prone zones

## Do Not Finish First

Do not fully coat these until fit is proven:

- miter-track dado shoulders
- carriage guide surfaces and stop faces
- carriage fence reference faces
- router-plate ledges
- saw mount interfaces
- overlay anchor points

Mask or scrape these surfaces after finish if needed.

## Sample Board Step

Before touching the bench:

1. Prepare offcuts of plywood, MDF, and construction lumber.
2. Ease the edges exactly how you plan to ease the bench.
3. Test shellac + waterborne finish schedule.
4. Confirm:
   - hand feel
   - sheen
   - stain resistance
   - whether MDF edges need a second seal coat

## Recommended Sequence

1. Machine and dry-fit everything.
2. Pre-finish interior panels that are hard to reach later.
3. Assemble the bench.
4. Final sand exposed faces.
5. Seal MDF.
6. Topcoat Class A surfaces.
7. Topcoat Class B surfaces.
8. Re-open critical fit surfaces if finish buildup tightens them.
